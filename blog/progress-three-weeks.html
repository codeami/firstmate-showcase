Three weeks in: what I built, what I checked, what is still open

A dated progress report on running a crew of AI workers, as of 14 October, with the evidence next to each claim and a 2 November update

Ashish Raina14 Oct 20265 min read

This is a dated list of what I built in my first three weeks, with the proof next to each claim. Where a number could not be checked, I left it out.

1. The idea, with a ship

A small boat has a captain and a first mate. The captain decides where to go. The mate hands out jobs to the crew, checks the work, and never lets the crew touch the charts without a say-so.

My setup is the same shape. I am the captain. One AI, called FirstMate, is my only point of contact. It sends other AI workers to do the work. Each works in its own throwaway copy of the project. FirstMate brings the results back for me to approve.

 me (captain)
   |
 FirstMate  --- keeps the queue, checks the work, asks me before anything permanent
   |
   +-- worker A (own copy)   +-- worker B (own copy)   +-- reviewer (reads only)

Test: the bin/fm-* scripts in FirstMate carry the rules. The list of workers and their states comes from bin/fm-crew-state.sh, not from anyone's memory.

3. A mistake I made, and how it was undone

When I set up the gate, the first run followed the "fork workflow" and opened a pull request on the original author's repository, not on my fork. It was caught the same day. The pull request was closed. The setting was pointed back at my fork. The work ran again there.

The lesson I take from it is small and useful: check where a command will send things before you run it, not after.

Test: the closed pull request and the changed origin are both visible in the project's remote settings.

4. "It passes its own tests" is not the same as "it works"

For taste-check I used mutation testing: a runner makes small deliberate breaks in the code and counts how many the tests catch. A test that still passes when the code is broken is only guarding itself.

Then I asked outside reviewers to read it cold. They found real bugs the tests had missed. Three of them:

  • an evidence pointer could reach outside its folder
  • a file link could reach outside the project folder
  • a check matched part of a longer token, like "150" inside "1500"

My reading: a perfect score on your own breaks only proves you thought of those breaks. Independent readers find the ones you did not. The fixes landed in two rounds, each with new tests and mutations.

Test: commits d2cb270 (9 October: "pointers stay in the root", "whole-token" matching) and aa14b1d (12 October: "hard links out of the root"). The mutation runner is tests/mutate-taste.py.

5. Cutting what loads before I say a word

Every AI session loads the short description of each skill I have installed, before my first message. Think of a toolbox where every tool shouts its name when you open the lid.

I counted 151 skills. My first estimate of the cost was about 8,300 tokens. The real figure, counting everything loaded before the first message, is closer to 26,500. I moved 60 skills into a quarantine folder (a backup came first, and nothing was destroyed). The other cuts wait until I see which skills the new chain actually names.

Caveat: these are estimates from byte counts, not an exact tokenizer. Test: the audit report shows the method and the backup checksum.

6. What was not done, as of 14 October

  • The other direction of the note flow: my saved preferences loading into every Claude session. It needs one dialog tested by a person.
  • Many commits in my public dotfiles repo existed only on my laptop. They needed a privacy read before anything was pushed.
  • Ideas researched but not built: a chat-plus-folders web app, a feed board, a travel chat, a call-recording receptionist.

Update, 2 November

  • Those dotfiles commits are now on GitHub. My copy is one commit ahead today.
  • backpass runs daily. It proposes rule edits, and I approve each one.
  • I reviewed a week of my own corrections, 46 of them, and approved 4 new rules: Your corrections are your next rules.
  • Three automatic checks are now in place: a secret scan at commit, a plain-language check on drafts, and a link check before replies. Why those three: Guides, sensors, and the rules I'm still begging for.
  • This site, with these Field Notes, is public.
