The story

How it works, from the very beginning

No AI knowledge needed. EA-bot explains one idea at a time. Pick a reply to go on.

Hi!

I'm EA-bot. Let's start at the very beginning. No jargon, I promise.

You may have chatted with an AI like ChatGPT or Claude. You type a message, and it writes back.

Underneath is a model: a program that has read a huge amount of text and is very good at writing what comes next.

On its own, a model only talks. It can't open your files, check its own work, or remember yesterday.

It needs a harness.

Think of a horse. The horse does the pulling. The harness makes the pulling go where you want.

For AI, the harness is everything around the model: the rules it reads, the tools it may use, the checks on its work, and the notes it keeps.

And that's me. I'm not the model. I'm the harness around it.

One helper doing everything is like one person running a whole office. It gets slow, and things get mixed up.

So the work is split. You talk only to me. I turn your ask into jobs and hand each job to a worker.

Each worker gets a written brief: one task, and what "done" looks like.

Each worker also gets its own copy of the work. Nobody trips over anybody, and your main copy stays safe.

Some workers find things out and write a report. Others change code and send it back as a pull request: a proposed change that waits for review.

First, each worker checks its own work. It runs the tests, and it backs each claim with proof.

Then automatic checks run before work leaves the machine: a review, tests, and a scan for secrets like passwords.

To be honest: some limits are scripts that can't be skipped, and some are still only written rules. Safety by design says which is which.

You're the captain. The crew does the work. You make the calls.

Only you can accept a change into the main copy, publish, send, delete, or spend money.

The crew can even suggest better rules for itself. It proposes. You approve each one. It never rewrites its own rules.

Not in my head. In files.

One short rules file, the map, is read first by every helper. Notes from each session are saved, and the next session starts from them.

Because it's all files, you can read exactly what each helper was told.

It's real. It's in daily use on the owner's machine. One of my workers built these pages.

On one day, four workers ran side by side, and three delivered a reviewed pull request: this site, reels4mphotos and swarm-referee.

Not everything works yet, and this site says so. What works today lists both.

The owner puts it this way: "managing a complex AI agentic bot system is sort of like managing a contract with different vendors."

You scope the work. You say who may do what. You accept work only on evidence.

That's the whole idea. Want more? Try chatting with me, read the Field Notes, or see How it works in more detail.

The supervisor and most crew mechanics are upstream Firstmate, MIT licence, by Kun Chen and contributors. The owner built the rules, hooks, work index and review flow.

by Ashish Raina – program manager turned AI-crew operator
